# Algebra-to-graph compiler bridge

Status: fixed tree evaluation supported; algebra/graph connection is established,
while computational leverage is rejected at this one-channel scale

## Capability and spine binding

Construct perturbative return kernels from the operations already generated by the
free-field algebra compiler, without importing a textbook list of diagrams,
symmetry factors, or fermionic signs.

- **Upstream anchor:** the natural-operation grammar, residual repairs, free
  CCR/CAR realization, observable quotient, and common observable-return calculus
  constructed in nodes 06, 07, 13, 14, 16, and 18.
- **Bridge question:** how does a certified local operation become an interaction
  vertex that can be composed into inequivalent perturbative graphs?
- **Invariant target:** the same prepared resolvent/self-energy and its
  coupling-visible spectral measure.
- **Downstream effect:** replace the single hand-constructed return in node 16 by a
  reusable perturbative constructor whose output can feed the same bound, temporal,
  and open-channel transforms.
- **Special resources:** a supplied interaction, free propagator or Green choice,
  perturbative order, regularization policy when needed, and a finite graph budget.
- **Rejoin edge:** a graph-summed self-energy or spectral contribution with exact
  combinatorial weight, algebra certificate, provenance, and refusal boundary.

Symmetry and the free algebra constrain admissible ports and contractions. They do
not select the interaction or the analytic boundary condition.

## The missing interface

The current executable grammar returns rewrite rules over operation words. A graph
compiler cannot safely consume raw tokens such as `A`, `P`, or `G`; their domains,
parity, and analytic meaning would be lost. The algebra compiler must retain the
following typed export:

```text
CompiledFieldDatum = {
  field colors and duals,
  parity of every color,
  physical quotient/projector,
  certified propagator pairing and its Green choice,
  certified local vertex maps from the supplied interaction,
  normal-form operation for internal fiber contractions,
  selection rules, certificates, and provenance
}
```

A vertex is not merely a coupling and valence. It is a map

```text
v: X_(c_1) tensor ... tensor X_(c_r) -> C
```

with typed ports, a permutation action on equal ports, total parity, and a
normal-form certificate. The supplied action or Hamiltonian generates the candidate
vertex; the algebra compiler checks and reduces it. This separates dynamics from
the representation-derived algebra while retaining both for graph evaluation.

Three budgets remain independent throughout the bridge:

```text
algebra depth  = maximum operation-word/rewrite depth,
coupling degree = number and types of interaction insertions,
loop degree     = E - V + C for the generated graph.
```

An order-`g` term contains one interaction insertion; it is neither one algebra
rewrite nor a one-loop graph. The graph compiler records all three degrees so that
an algebraic refusal, perturbative truncation, and loop truncation cannot be
mistaken for one another.

## Constructing graphs rather than drawing them

For a multiplicity vector `n=(n_v)` of admitted vertex types, first construct the
finite set `Hist(n)` of typed contraction histories: copies of vertex ports,
external ports fixed by the query, and admissible pairings of the remaining ports.
The relabelling group

```text
G_n = product_v (Aut(v)^(n_v) semidirect S_(n_v))
```

acts by permuting identical vertices and their equivalent ports. Two histories have
the same physical contraction pattern precisely when they lie in the same orbit.
The compiler therefore defines a graph to be an orbit

```text
Gamma = G_n dot h,  h in Hist(n),
```

with external labels and field colors retained. For a bosonic history, evaluation
is constant on its orbit. Orbit--stabilizer computes

```text
1/|G_n| sum_(h' in G_n dot h) Ev(h')
  = Ev(h)/|Stab_(G_n)(h)|
  = Ev(Gamma)/|Aut_ext(Gamma)|.
```

Thus the symmetry factor is generated by the quotient; it is not an extra
Feynman rule. For odd ports, moving the contracted fields to the compiler's
canonical order contributes the Koszul character

```text
chi(h)=(-1)^(number of odd transpositions).
```

The signed evaluation `chi(h) Ev(h)` is used in the same orbit calculation. If an
automorphism reverses the induced fermionic orientation, the orbit contribution
cancels rather than receiving an invented sign. Open fermion lines retain the
external order; closed fermion cycles acquire their sign from the same operation.

After quotienting, the algebra normalizer evaluates internal finite-fiber
contractions once per graph. Only then does an analytic evaluator attach momentum,
position, resolvent, retarded, advanced, or Euclidean kernels. The common graph is
combinatorial; these evaluators are different semantic functors and cannot be
silently interchanged.

## The existing coupling-order bridge

The companion paper supplies

```text
H(g)=H_0+g V,  P=P_prep,  Q=1-P,
Q H_0 P=0,  P V P=0.
```

The first equality computes the departure map

```text
B(g)=Q H(g) P
    =Q H_0 P+g Q V P
    =g B^(1),
```

so the **transition vertex** is order `g`. It is not yet an order-`g` prepared
observable. A prepared-to-prepared graph with one such vertex has unmatched field
parity and is refused; equivalently `PVP=0`.

At degree two, the graph request fixes two external prepared ports. Vacuum and
sector selection leave one admissible connected return history:

```text
P --g B^(1)--> Q --R_0(z)--> Q --g (B^(1))^dagger--> P.
```

Its external labels fix the direction, hence its external automorphism group is
trivial. The graph evaluator returns

```text
Sigma^(2)(z)
  = g^2 (B^(1))^dagger R_0(z) B^(1),
R_0(z)=(z-QH_0Q)^(-1).
```

Applying the spectral resolution of the **same** internal propagator gives

```text
M^(2)(Delta)
  = g^2 (B^(1))^dagger E_(QH_0Q)(Delta) B^(1).
```

This is exactly the measure consumed by node 16. The algebra compiler supplies the
typed vertex and reduces its physical fiber; the graph compiler supplies the
composition, coupling degree, multiplicity, and sign; the observable compiler
supplies the spectral quotient and transforms. None can replace the other.

## Textbook baseline and possible advantage

The textbook route is usually

```text
Lagrangian -> remembered Feynman rules -> draw selected diagrams
  -> assign symmetry/sign factors -> expand indices or gamma words
  -> evaluate integrals -> recover an observable.
```

The proposed route is

```text
certified free algebra + supplied interaction + observable request
  -> typed vertices and contraction histories
  -> graph-isomorphism quotient and parity sign
  -> one algebraic normal form per graph
  -> analytic kernel DAG
  -> the same requested observable.
```

At order `g^2` with one surviving graph, this offers provenance and a regression
bridge, not a runtime advantage. The potential leverage begins when many Wick
histories collapse to the same colored graph:

- enumerate graph orbits rather than all `(2E-1)!!` pairings;
- reduce internal representation/fiber algebra once per graph rather than once per
  contraction history;
- generate bosonic, fermionic, and mixed signs from one parity rule;
- reuse connected, 1PI, resolvent, and boundary evaluations of the same graph IR;
- discard gauge, trace, blind-observable, and forbidden-statistics channels before
  integral construction;
- cache repeated subgraphs and integral kernels across several observables.

Graph canonicalization, invariant-map construction, and observable recovery have
costs of their own. A hand calculation may remain cheaper for one or two diagrams.
The compiler does not make divergent integrals finite, prove perturbative
convergence, construct a dressed bound state, or turn a boundary value into an
`S` matrix. It unifies the **generation and algebraic reduction of perturbative
terms**, not every analytic or physical problem in QFT.

Wick expansion, linked-cluster extraction, 1PI decomposition, Dyson resummation,
and graph symmetry factors are already standard QFT machinery. Reproducing them
with software is a regression backend, not the research contribution. The combined
compiler changes the present spine only if it demonstrates at least one stronger
capability:

- the physical quotient or observable annihilator generated upstream removes
  forbidden graph families before they are enumerated;
- residual repair generates an admissible vertex/source adapter that the graph
  layer can reuse rather than receive as a memorized rule;
- the same graph-summed kernel feeds both the bound resolvent and continuum
  boundary/visible-measure route without reconstructing its finite-fiber algebra;
- the complete route measurably reduces histories, algebra-normalization calls,
  retained expression size, recovery depth, or runtime for the same observable.

If none of these occurs, the graph layer remains useful engineering but should be
classified as a conventional perturbative backend rather than the paper's
generative thesis.

## First exploration: why the curvature return is not discriminating

Rank two remains the first useful carrier because trace first appears there. Yet
the detector coupling proposed above is too well adapted to the quotient to test
the trace repair. For its curvature covector `e_f`, construct

```text
J_e=K_2^dagger e_f.
```

The generated annihilators calculate both constraints:

```text
P_p^dagger J_e=(K_2 P_p)^dagger e_f=0,
T J_e=U^dagger K_2^dagger e_f=(K_2 U)^dagger e_f=0.
```

At spin two the generated source adapter is

```text
M_2^(-1)=I-U T/4.
```

Applying it to the same source gives

```text
M_2^(-1)J_e
  =J_e-U T J_e/4
  =J_e.
```

Equivalently, any curvature readout satisfies `K_2 M_2^(-1)=K_2`. Therefore the
degree-two detector return and its `E^5` density remain valid regressions for the
observable quotient, coupling order, and measure compiler, but they cannot show
that a graph evaluator uses the trace/source repair. The unique graph has trivial
external automorphism and no competing contraction history. Building a general
graph compiler around it would add machinery without a combinatorial capability.

## Active initiation bench: traceful spin-two exchange

The smallest graph that cannot discard the generated adapter is a two-ended
potential exchange between conserved sources with nonzero trace. Construct such a
source without spacetime components. Work on an off-shell momentum region
`Q_p != 0` and let `sigma` be a scalar source. The two natural rank-two lifts are

```text
u_0=U sigma,
u_1=P_p^2 Q_p^(-1) sigma.
```

The generated grammar evaluates divergence on this same two-dimensional invariant
cell. Since `A_p sigma=T sigma=0`, its exchange relations compute

```text
A_p u_0
  =(U A_p+2P_p)sigma
  =2P_p sigma,

A_p u_1
  =Q_p^(-1) A_p P_p^2 sigma
  =Q_p^(-1)(P_p^2 A_p+2Q_p P_p)sigma
  =2P_p sigma.
```

The trace relations calculate independently

```text
T u_0
  =(U T+2d)sigma
  =8 sigma,

T u_1
  =Q_p^(-1)(P_p^2 T+4P_p A_p+2Q_p)sigma
  =2 sigma
```

Hence the divergence row is `(2,2)`. Its kernel constructs, rather than assumes,
the conserved traceful source

```text
J_sigma=u_0-u_1,
A_p J_sigma=0,
T J_sigma=6 sigma != 0.
```

The invariant pairing follows from `U^dagger=T` and `P_p^dagger=A_p`. Evaluate all
four entries on the same scalar pair `(tau,sigma)`:

```text
<U tau,U sigma>=<tau,TU sigma>=8<tau,sigma>,
<U tau,P_p^2 Q_p^(-1)sigma>
  =<tau,T P_p^2 Q_p^(-1)sigma>
  =2<tau,sigma>,
<P_p^2 Q_p^(-1)tau,U sigma>
  =<tau,Q_p^(-1)A_p^2 U sigma>
  =2<tau,sigma>,
<P_p^2 Q_p^(-1)tau,P_p^2 Q_p^(-1)sigma>
  =<tau,Q_p^(-2)A_p^2 P_p^2 sigma>
  =2<tau,sigma>.
```

Thus on the ordered basis `(u_0,u_1)` the finite Gram factor is

```text
G=matrix(8,2; 2,2).
```

The adapter coefficient is generated independently. On the spin-two trace cell,
`(UT)^2=8UT`. Composing the known adapter `M=I-UT/4` with the smallest inverse
candidate `I+nUT` leaves

```text
M(I+nUT)-I=(-1/4-n)UT.
```

The vanishing residual forces `n=-1/4`, rather than importing trace reversal. Put
`h=(1,-1)^T`, the coordinates of `J_sigma`. The unadapted and compiled internal
edges now evaluate on the same source:

```text
h^T G h=6,
h^T G M_2^(-1)h=-3.
```

Restoring the scalar Green operation, the graph-layer normal form is therefore

```text
E_2(tau,sigma)
  =g_2^2 <J_tau,G_Q M_2^(-1)J_sigma>
  =-3g_2^2 <tau,G_Q sigma>,
```

while omitting the generated repair would return
`6g_2^2 <tau,G_Q sigma>`. The sign and coefficient change prove that this source
cell genuinely consumes the field-equation compiler output.

The graph request remains bounded: generate the single two-vertex exchange edge,
attach the compiled `M_2^(-1)` normal form, and preserve the same retarded,
advanced, Euclidean, or resolvent choice carried by `G_Q`. It may not receive a
hard-coded graviton numerator or component projector.

This is an algebra-use certificate, not yet a physical scattering prediction. The
constructed probe uses `Q_p^(-1)` and is singular on the null shell, so it is an
off-shell invariant test source rather than a local matter current. Its compact
return to the spine is the reusable edge operation

```text
(J_L,J_R,G_Q) -> <J_L,G_Q M_2^(-1)J_R>
```

with conservation, trace, adapter, boundary, and provenance certificates.

## Local scalar vertex generated by its gauge obstruction

The next consumer must be local, otherwise the preceding source remains only an
algebra probe. Supply a scalar matter dispersion

```text
E(p)=Q_p-m^2,
E(p)=E(p')=0,
q=p'-p,
s=<p,p'>.
```

The scalar dynamics supplies the on-shell equalities; it does not supply a
spin-two vertex. The cheapest local symmetric rank-two bilinear is
`V_0=P_p P_(p') 1`. Contracting it with the transfer momentum on the same pair of
on-shell inputs computes its gauge obstruction:

```text
A_q V_0
  =<q,p>P_(p')+<q,p'>P_p
  =(s-m^2)P_(p')+(m^2-s)P_p
  =(s-m^2)P_q.
```

The metric resource generates the lowest-degree correction channel. On a scalar,

```text
A_q[U(s-m^2)]=2(s-m^2)P_q.
```

Consequently the exact residual solve forces coefficient `-1/2` and returns

```text
J_0(p',p)=P_p P_(p')1-U(s-m^2)/2,
A_q J_0(p',p)=0.
```

Its trace is evaluated, rather than presumed:

```text
T J_0=2s-d(s-m^2).
```

In four dimensions this is `4m^2-2s`, so the compiled spin-two source adapter is
generically visible on this local matter vertex.

Conservation does not make the vertex unique. The same grammar constructs an
identically conserved local operation

```text
I_q=P_q^2-U Q_q,
A_q I_q=2Q_q P_q-2Q_q P_q=0.
```

The admissible family is therefore

```text
J_xi=J_0+xi I_q,
A_q J_xi=0,
T J_xi=2s-d(s-m^2)+2(1-d)xi Q_q.
```

The algebra compiler must retain `xi`; it cannot select it from conservation. A
supplied minimal or improved matter action owns that choice. This is a useful
refusal boundary: representation and gauge compatibility generate the vertex
family, while matter dynamics selects a member.

## First graph orbit generated from the typed vertex

Give the graph layer two copies of a vertex with port colors

```text
(scalar,scalar,spin-two)
```

and request coupling degree two with four labelled external scalar ports. The two
spin-two ports must contract internally. Quotienting assignments by exchange of
the two scalar ports at each vertex and by exchange of the identical vertices
leaves the unordered pair partitions

```text
{{1,2},{3,4}},
{{1,3},{2,4}},
{{1,4},{2,3}}.
```

These are the three familiar channels only after interpretation; they were not
compiler inputs. With the symmetric vertex normalization already included, every
external-label-fixed graph has trivial remaining automorphism and the same relative
weight. The generated amputated tree kernel is

```text
A_4^(2)
  =g_2^2 sum_({{i,j},{k,l}})
     <J_xi(p_j,p_i),G_Q M_2^(-1)J_xi(p_l,p_k)>.
```

This expression is the first actual connection of the two compilers: the graph
quotient generates the three compositions, while the algebra compiler supplies
and normalizes every vertex and internal edge. The analytic evaluator still owes
the Green boundary, crossing convention, external normalization, and a fixed
kinematic evaluation before this becomes a scattering observable.

## Fixed-kinematics evaluation and baseline

To compare the same object without crossing or identical-particle normalization
ambiguities, color the two scalar lines differently and select the generated
transfer orbit. Use signature `(+---)`, minimal matter coupling `xi=0`, equal mass
`m=1`, center-of-mass momentum magnitude `k=1/2`, and right-angle scattering. Then

```text
E=sqrt(m^2+k^2)=sqrt(5)/2,
s_M=5,
t_M=(p_3-p_1)^2=-1/2,
u_M=4m^2-s_M-t_M=-1/2.
```

For this orbit put

```text
a=(s_M-2m^2)/2=3/2,
b=m^2-u_M/2=5/4.
```

The invariant grammar and pairings above evaluate the two current factors without
materializing a rank-four propagator:

```text
<J_13,J_24>
  =a^2+b^2+m^2 t_M
  =53/16,

T J_13=T J_24
  =2m^2+t_M
  =3/2.
```

Applying the compiled edge now gives

```text
N_alg
  =<J_13,M_2^(-1)J_24>
  =53/16-(1/4)(3/2)^2
  =11/4,

A_t^(2)/g_2^2
  =N_alg/t_M
  =-11/2.
```

The independent textbook baseline constructs the component current

```text
J^(mu nu)=p^mu p'^nu+p'^mu p^nu
  -eta^(mu nu)(<p,p'>-m^2)
```

and contracts both copies with the de Donder numerator. Direct evaluation on the
same four momenta gives

```text
N_dD=J_13:J_24-(1/2)(tr J_13)(tr J_24)=11/2.
```

The tensor-to-polynomial convention used by the algebra compiler is
`Phi(u)=h_(mu nu)u^mu u^nu/2`, so its invariant carrier pairing is one half of the
full component contraction. Therefore

```text
N_alg=N_dD/2=11/4.
```

The remaining factor belongs to the declared vertex-coupling normalization, not a
physical discrepancy. The executable certificate independently performs the
four-dimensional contraction; components are used only for this local baseline.

The complete-route comparison is negative at this scale. Once the textbook vertex
and de Donder propagator are known, a symmetry-aware one-channel calculation is as
short as the invariant one and avoids the compiler's construction cost. The new
route does provide provenance, exposes the improvement freedom, and reuses one
edge across generated channels, but none of those facts establishes arithmetic or
runtime reduction for this tree.

This branch therefore stops with a **supported connector** and a **rejected tree-
level leverage claim**. Node 19 selects the bounded order-`g^2` scalar two-point
request. Its first off-shell computation shows that the cubic-only loop is not
Ward closed, so action-generated contact completion must precede regularization or
integration. Fermionic signs and Connes--Kreimer reduction remain outside this
tree node's horizon.

## Checks, support, and refusal

The active bench is supported only if all of the following hold:

- the invariant source cell, rather than a named stress tensor or graviton rule, is
  the test input;
- exact kernel computation generates `J_sigma`, and direct operation evaluation
  certifies both `A_p J_sigma=0` and `T J_sigma=6 sigma`;
- the residual solver generates the inverse-adapter coefficient and certifies that
  the composition residual vanishes;
- the repaired edge evaluates to `-3` while the otherwise identical unadapted edge
  evaluates to `6`;
- a curvature readout independently reduces the same adapter to the identity,
  preserving the earlier quotient regression but rejecting it as an adapter-use
  bench;
- the scalar on-shell residual generates `J_0`, and the identically conserved
  `I_q` exposes rather than hides the action-owned improvement freedom;
- quotienting two typed scalar--scalar--spin-two vertices generates exactly three
  labelled external graph orbits without channel names as input;
- the fixed right-angle transfer evaluates to `N_alg=11/4`, and the independent
  de Donder component contraction returns `N_dD=11/2`, exactly matching the
  compiler's one-half carrier-pairing convention;
- the whole-route verdict records no computational advantage for one tree channel;
- missing Green choice, invalid source class, incompatible ports, exceeded algebra
  or coupling degree, or failed normal-form coincidence returns a typed refusal.

No graph-enumeration or runtime advantage is claimed at one edge. Reject the bridge
if its implementation merely hard-codes the final rank-two numerator, or if a
local matter-current transfer cannot reuse the returned edge operation.
