## The pole obstruction generates a resolution algebra

Let `H_Q` be the self-adjoint unresolved generator from node 18, with spectral
family `E_Q`. A detector that distinguishes only whether the excess invariant mass
is at most `Delta` constructs the effect

```text
D_Delta=E_Q([mu,mu+Delta]),             Delta>0.             (23.1)
```

This is not an arbitrary smearing convention. The reported event is the interval,
and functional calculus turns that event into its unique spectral projector. For
two resolutions, direct multiplication of their indicator functions computes

```text
D_Delta D_Lambda
 =1_[mu,mu+Delta](H_Q) 1_[mu,mu+Lambda](H_Q)
 =1_[mu,mu+min(Delta,Lambda)](H_Q)
 =D_min(Delta,Lambda).                                      (23.2)
```

Thus finite resolutions form a nested effect algebra. In particular,
`0<Delta<=Lambda` gives `D_Delta D_Lambda=D_Delta`: the finer report is a
subevent of the coarser acceptance.

For a retained preparation `eta` and departure map `B`, evaluate the effect on the
same departure vector:

```text
R_Delta(eta)
 =<B eta,D_Delta B eta>
 =<eta,B^dagger E_Q([mu,mu+Delta])B eta>
 =<eta,M_B([mu,mu+Delta])eta>.                              (23.3)
```

Equation (23.3) is the semantic bridge: detector response and visible-measure mass
are the same composite on the same preparation. No particle pole occurs in its
construction.

## Construct the minimal visible realization

The graph calculation has not yet supplied the absolute cut normalization or a
gauge-invariant field detector. It has supplied a positive spectral shape. On a
finite accepted band `[mu,mu+Lambda]`, that shape is integrable, so it constructs a
minimal conditional realization without a full Fock-space state.

For a positive density `rho` define

```text
J(Lambda)=integral_mu^(mu+Lambda) rho(s) d s,

H_Lambda=L^2([mu,mu+Lambda],d s),
(H_vis f)(s)=s f(s),

psi_Lambda(s)
 =1_[mu,mu+Lambda](s) sqrt(rho(s)/J(Lambda)).                (23.4)
```

The same integral computes

```text
||psi_Lambda||^2
 =1/J(Lambda) integral_mu^(mu+Lambda) rho(s) d s
 =1.                                                        (23.5)
```

Multiplication by `1_[mu,mu+Delta]` realizes `D_Delta`. For
`0<Delta<=Lambda`, its probability on the constructed cyclic vector is

```text
F(Delta|Lambda)
 =<psi_Lambda,D_Delta psi_Lambda>
 =J(Delta)/J(Lambda).                                      (23.6)
```

This construction does not assert that `psi_Lambda` is the gravitationally dressed
scalar. It is the minimal cyclic representative of exactly the distinctions visible
to the nested detector effects. Any field or asymptotic-state realization with the
same measure gives the same values of (23.6).

## Exact threshold response generated by the packet

In four dimensions node 22 returns

```text
rho_Sigma(s)=2 mu (2s-mu)(1-mu/s),          s>=mu.           (23.7)
```

Put `x=s-mu`. Then both factors are computed on the same threshold displacement:

```text
rho_Sigma(mu+x)
 =2 mu (mu+2x) x/(mu+x)
 =2 mu [2x-mu+mu^2/(mu+x)].                                 (23.8)
```

Integrating (23.8), rather than importing a tabulated detector law, gives

```text
J_mu(Delta)
 =2 mu integral_0^Delta [2x-mu+mu^2/(mu+x)] d x
 =2 mu [Delta^2-mu Delta+mu^2 log(1+Delta/mu)].              (23.9)
```

Differentiating the constructed answer returns its input:

```text
dJ_mu/dDelta
 =2 mu [2Delta-mu+mu^2/(mu+Delta)]
 =rho_Sigma(mu+Delta).                                     (23.10)
```

Because (23.8) is nonnegative for `x>=0`, `J_mu` is positive and monotone. If the
action/cut convention later supplies an overall positive factor `N`, the absolute
cell response is `N J_mu(Delta)`. The conditional observable

```text
F_mu(Delta|Lambda)
 =J_mu(Delta)/J_mu(Lambda)                                 (23.11)
```

cancels `N` exactly. Local subtraction polynomials also have no continuum
discontinuity and therefore do not enter either `J_mu` or (23.11).

Expansion is used only to expose the threshold law after the exact construction:

```text
J_mu(Delta)
 =mu Delta^2+(2/3)Delta^3-(1/(2mu))Delta^4
  +O(Delta^5/mu^2),

F_mu(Delta|Lambda)
 =(Delta/Lambda)^2 [1+O((Delta+Lambda)/mu)]                 (23.12)
```

when both resolutions are small compared with `mu`. The quadratic resolution law
is the first parameter-free spectral prediction of the active graph branch. Node
24 shows that it does not yet replace the undefined pole residue by a physical
observable: the bare window fails the packet's external quotient.

## What has been reconstructed

The failed intuition was

```text

```


This candidate reconstruction changes the primitive question from “which vector

Coherent states, memory sectors, or an algebraic scattering construction must now

## Retained operation and certificates

The workbench extends the existing spectral compiler:

```text
CompileThresholdWindow(boundary,Delta,Lambda,N,d)
  -> interval [mu,mu+Delta]

   + conditional fraction J(Delta)/J(Lambda)
   + positivity/nesting domain

```

Its inputs contain the detector resolutions and normalization, not the expected
closed form. Certificates check:

- exact differentiation of the cell mass back to the compiled density;
- the quadratic four-dimensional threshold coefficient;
- cancellation of normalization in the nested fraction;
- strict containment `0<F(Delta|Lambda)<1` on a numerical transfer case; and
- refusals for nonpositive resolution or normalization, reversed containment, and
  dimensions in which the spin-two density is not positive at threshold.
