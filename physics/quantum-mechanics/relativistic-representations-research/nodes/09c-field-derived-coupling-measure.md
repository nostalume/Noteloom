## Research contract and stopping bench

- **Question:** can `M_B(Delta)=B^dagger E_Q(Delta)B` be constructed from an
  actual field Hamiltonian and preparation without solving its complete dressed
  Fock problem, and can the same constructed object compute a bound observable
  and an open-channel observable?
- **Presumptions:** three spatial dimensions; a scalar translation-invariant
  particle; a massive scalar boson; radial Gaussian ultraviolet cutoff; vacuum
  preparation; fixed total momentum near zero; and sufficiently small coupling
  for a gapped even expansion.
- **Output:** the complete coefficient of order `g^2`, not a nonperturbative
  formula in disguise. It must recover energy shift, prepared residue, effective
  mass curvature, memory, threshold tail, continuum boundary, and a target-limited
  moment chain from one field-derived measure.
- **Internal stop:** do not add internal atomic levels, transverse photons,
  massless limits, higher Fock orders, or a multichannel `S` matrix unless this
  same-model benchmark fails. It passes below, so those remain re-entry branches.

The answer is therefore precise:

```text
exact finite g: not without resolving the interacting cyclic Fock sector;
order g^2: yes, because the departure and first return are exactly one-boson;
open boundary: yes as channel information, not yet as a full scattering matrix.
```

## 1. Construct the field model and the preparation

Let

```text
F=Gamma_s(L^2(R^3)),
omega(k)=sqrt(|k|^2+mu^2),
P_f=dGamma(k),
H_f=dGamma(omega).
```

For total momentum `p`, construct the fiber Hamiltonian

```text
H_g(p)=(p-P_f)^2/(2M)+H_f+g Phi(h),             (1.1)
Phi(h)=a^dagger(h)+a(h),
h(k)=exp(-|k|^2/(2 Lambda^2)).
```

The mass `mu>0` opens a one-boson gap, and the Gaussian makes every integral used
below finite. Choose the field vacuum `chi=Omega` and construct

```text
P=|Omega><Omega|,   Q=1-P.                       (1.2)
```

This is a field model, not a prescribed Friedrichs density. Its Fock sectors,
recoil energy, dispersion, and coupling determine the density later.

At `p=0`, both the free particle term and `H_f` annihilate `Omega`, while
`a(h)Omega=0`. Hence the departure map is computed rather than named:

```text
B=QH_g(0)P,
B Omega=g a^dagger(h)Omega.                      (1.3)
```

## 2. Reduce exactly before approximating

Rotations commute with `H_g(0)` because `omega` and `h` are radial. Equation
(1.3) is a rotation scalar, so every non-scalar angular channel is absent from
the cyclic sector generated by `B Omega`. At the first nonzero return order,
boson-number selection is stronger: `B Omega` has exactly one boson, and its free
cyclic evolution remains in the radial one-boson channel.

The exact complementary operator is

```text
H_Q(g)=QH_g(0)Q.                                 (2.1)
```

It couples one boson to two, two to three, and so on. Thus writing
`B^dagger E_(H_Q(g)) B` does **not** remove the complete Fock problem. Field
parity `(-1)^N` sends `g` to `-g`, fixes `P`, and changes the sign of `B`; the two
signs from the external `B` factors cancel. For off-axis resolvents, moments on
their common domains, and sufficiently regular spectral test functions, the
gapped expansion is therefore even:

```text
integral f(x)dM_B(x)
 =g^2 integral f(x)dM_2(x)+O(g^4).               (2.2)
```

This is deliberately not a setwise expansion for every Borel projection:
continuum spectral projections need not vary analytically at sharp endpoints.
`g^2 M_2` below denotes the leading measure that generates the perturbative return
transforms.

The coefficient `M_2` uses only the free complement. On one boson,

```text
H_Q(0) -> epsilon(k)=omega(k)+|k|^2/(2M).         (2.3)
```

Apply its spectral projection to the actual departure vector:

```text
g^2 M_2(Delta)
 =g^2 <a^dagger(h)Omega,
       E_(H_Q(0))(Delta)a^dagger(h)Omega>
 =g^2 integral_(R^3) 1_Delta(epsilon(k))
       |h(k)|^2 d^3k.                            (2.4)
```

Equation (2.4) is the promised field-derived measure. No dressed eigenvector and
no Fock truncation diagonalization has entered; only the perturbative return
order, symmetry, and the spectral action of the free one-boson field were used.

## 3. Push the radial channel to energy without losing its origin

Put `r=|k|`. The energy

```text
epsilon(r)=sqrt(r^2+mu^2)+r^2/(2M)               (3.1)
```

is strictly increasing for `r>0`. If `x=epsilon(r)`, first recover

```text
omega(x)=-M+sqrt(M^2+mu^2+2Mx),
r(x)=sqrt(omega(x)^2-mu^2).                      (3.2)
```

The shell volume `4 pi r^2 dr` and the Jacobian

```text
epsilon'(r)=r(1/omega(r)+1/M)                    (3.3)
```

then construct the density

```text
dM(x)=m(x)dx,
m(x)=4 pi g^2 r(x)^2 |h(r(x))|^2/epsilon'(r(x)),
x>mu.                                            (3.4)
```

This change of variable preserves the measure: it does not replace the field
with a guessed bath. For the benchmark `M=2`, `mu=Lambda=1`, `g=0.2`, both routes
give

```text
M(R)=g^2 integral |h(k)|^2 d^3k
    =g^2 pi^(3/2) Lambda^3
    =0.22273311987326... .                        (3.5)
```

## 4. One Stieltjes transform gives three bound quantities

The order-`g^2` self-energy is the Stieltjes transform of (2.4):

```text
Sigma_2(z)=integral dM(x)/(z-x).                 (4.1)
```

The Feshbach equation at `p=0` is

```text
E=Sigma_2(E)+O(g^4).                             (4.2)
```

Expanding the right side at the bare energy gives the complete order-`g^2` field
coefficients

```text
Delta E^(2)=Sigma_2(0)=-integral dM(x)/x,
Z=1-integral dM(x)/x^2+O(g^4).                  (4.3)
```

The second equality follows constructively: the complementary component is
`(E-H_Q)^(-1)B Omega`; its squared norm is the integral in (4.3), and normalizing
`Omega` plus that component gives the prepared spectral weight.

For comparison only, solving (4.2) with the leading measure unexpanded gives a
one-boson resummation. It is not promoted to an exact full-Fock result.

The same measure also computes curvature, but an important semantic condition is
needed. At nonzero total momentum,

```text
epsilon_p(k)=(p-k)^2/(2M)+omega(k).
```

Differentiate the order-`g^2` pole equation twice at `p=0`. The odd term vanishes
by rotations, and angular averaging gives

```text
1/M_eff
 =1/M-(2/(3M^2)) integral r(x)^2/x^3 dM(x)+O(g^4).   (4.4)
```

Here the scalar measure is sufficient only because (3.1) is injective, so the
discarded radius is reconstructed by (3.2). With several bands, anisotropy, or a
noninjective dispersion, effective mass requires a joint/operator-valued measure
retaining momentum response; `M_B` alone would be insufficient.

The regression obtains

```text
Delta E^(2)=-0.128671982921048,
Z^(2)=0.918875728422823,
M_eff^(2)=2.026662224335467.                     (4.5)
```

The leading-measure resummation gives pole `-0.119687966633272` and residue
`0.935036880922097`. Directly reciprocating the truncated inverse mass gives
`2.027022463831858`. These three resummed numbers are diagnostics, not
higher-order claims.

## 5. The same measure exposes the open channel and its memory

For `E>mu`, the boundary of (4.1) is computed by the Poisson-kernel limit:

```text
Sigma_2(E+i0)
 =PV integral dM(x)/(E-x)-i pi m(E),
-2 Im Sigma_2(E+i0)=2 pi m(E).                  (5.1)
```

At `E=1.5`, the benchmark gives

```text
m(1.5)=0.164138506388275,
2 pi m(1.5)=1.031312651681211.                  (5.2)
```

This is open-channel loss available to a level or incoming channel at that
energy. It is not by itself a decay probability or cross section: those require
the physical preparation, reference dynamics, flux normalization, and wave
operators. The vacuum pole of section 4 remains below the threshold and does not
decay.

The real-time return is another transform of precisely (2.4):

```text
K(t)=B^dagger exp(-itH_Q(0))B
    =integral exp(-itx)dM(x).                    (5.3)
```

Near `r=0`, construct rather than quote

```text
epsilon(r)=mu+a r^2+O(r^4),
a=(1/2)(1/mu+1/M).
```

Substitution into (3.4) gives

```text
m(mu+y)=C sqrt(y)+O(y^(3/2)),
C=2 pi g^2/a^(3/2).                              (5.4)
```

Fourier scaling `u=ty` then evaluates the endpoint integral:

```text
K(t)
 =A exp(-imu t-3pi i/4)t^(-3/2)+O(t^(-5/2)),
A=pi^(3/2)g^2/a^(3/2).                           (5.5)
```

At `t=80`, the computed scaled coefficient differs from (5.5) by `5.01e-3`.
Thus the continuum boundary and long memory are two readable faces of the same
threshold geometry.

## 6. Moment chain: useful approximation with a visible refusal

Repeated free-channel action constructs

```text
mu_n=integral x^n dM(x).                         (6.1)
```

Gram--Schmidt on `B Omega,H_QB Omega,...` produces the Jacobi chain. The first
two sites are determined by

```text
b_0=mu_1/mu_0,
a_1^2=mu_2/mu_0-b_0^2,
b_1=(mu_3-2b_0mu_2+b_0^2mu_1)/(mu_0a_1^2).      (6.2)
```

For the benchmark,

```text
(b_0,a_1,b_1)=(1.91580722994,0.659268336925,2.73528781730),
nodes=(1.54932471519,3.10177033206).
```

Its two-atom measure reproduces `mu_0,...,mu_3` to `1.2e-16` or better. It misses
`mu_4` by `0.1069`, but approximates the off-axis self-energy at `z=-5` within
`4.69e-6`. This is the intended result: finite moments support finite-time or
far-off-axis work, not a continuum threshold or irreversible tail.

## 7. Route coincidence and complete construction cost

The isolated computation is
[documented here](../computation/09c-field-derived-measure/README.md). It checks
the following same-input diagram:

```text
field H_g + vacuum P
  -> B=QH_gP
  -> rotation + first-return reduction
  -> field pushforward M
       |-> Stieltjes: energy, residue, curvature
       |-> Fourier: memory and threshold tail
       |-> moments: finite Jacobi chain
       `-> real boundary: open-channel loss
  -> compare transforms on the same measure
  -> audit construction plus recovery cost.
```

| Task | Complete dressed route | N9c route | What paid for the gain |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| construct exact `M_B(g)` | interacting cyclic Fock spectral problem | not attempted | no exact compression available |
| coefficient through `g^2` | unnecessarily solves all sectors | one radial one-boson quadrature | field parity and one-boson first return |
| bound shift/residue | dressed eigenpair and overlap | two Stieltjes moments | gap keeps evaluation off spectrum |
| effective mass | momentum-dependent dressed eigenbranch | one weighted Stieltjes moment | rotations and injective radial dispersion |
| memory/tail | full time evolution | one Fourier integral plus endpoint law | same cyclic measure and threshold expansion |
| continuum boundary | limiting full resolvent | pointwise density | explicit pushforward; not a full `S` matrix |
| finite-time/off-axis approximation | repeated full propagation/solves | short Jacobi chain | accepts target-local error and recurrence |

The semantic quotient to the coupling-visible sector is exact, but its interacting
spectral construction is still infinite. The actual computational compression
comes from combining that quotient with perturbative order, boson-number
selection, rotations, locality of the Gaussian coupling in momentum, and a
target-specific transform. No single ingredient would justify the result alone.

## 8. Global verdict, stop, and re-entry

This node closes N9b's first field probe at the declared internal benchmark:

- an actual field Hamiltonian and preparation construct the measure rather than
  receiving it as external input;
- one measure recovers the order-`g^2` bound energy, prepared residue, mass
  curvature, memory kernel, threshold exponent, and open boundary;
- radial and energy-density Fourier routes agree within `5.3e-15` at `t=0` and
  more tightly at the tested positive times;
- the finite chain succeeds only on its promised moments and far-off-axis target;
- the full-Fock problem is avoided only to order `g^2`, and this limitation is
  part of the result.

The branch stops here. Re-enter only if the global spine needs one of these
capabilities:

1. **physical atom:** restore `H_at` and compute its operator-valued one-boson
   measure;
2. **massless radiation:** replace the artificial gap by uniform infrared
   estimates and distinguish ground states from infraparticles;
3. **finite coupling:** use recursive Fock-sector/continued-fraction structure or
   certified many-body numerics, then compare against the present coefficient;
4. **scattering observable:** add a declared incoming channel and wave operators,
   then recover an `S`-matrix element rather than renaming (5.1);
5. **noninjective or anisotropic channel:** retain a joint momentum-energy or
   operator-valued measure before computing response curvature.
