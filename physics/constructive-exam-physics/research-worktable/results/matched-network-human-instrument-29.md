# Result 29: matched network human instrument

## Tension

`C-network-human-v1` derives an exact break-even region, but it cannot identify
human action weights or show that learners can execute the sparse rule after the
strip is removed. Reusing the older micro-tool instrument would also be invalid:
that frozen contract joins delay and unseen variation in one near-family phase and
hard-codes four unrelated operator families.

The missing object was therefore not another cognitive argument. It was a
network-specific acquisition contract that makes immediate assistance, delayed
retention, and genuinely changed topology separately observable.

## Construction `C-network-study-v1`

The frozen bench contains six matched pairs and twelve exact numerical variants:

| Phase | Pairs | Topology relation | Strip |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| `immediate_assisted` | 2 | two- and three-leaf trained stars | sparse trials only |
| `delayed_same_topology` | 2 | same two families, new coefficients/boundaries | absent |
| `unseen_topology` | 2 | split-hub and branch-chain families | absent |

Every prompt asks for the same observable: current injected at the left boundary
under fixed terminal voltages. The executable key condenses the full balance law,
extracts the first boundary injection, and certifies equality with both declared
and sparse scalar routes. All twelve cases lie in the conditional structural
class; this instrument measures execution where reduced update work is possible,
not the already-proved one-hidden direct gate.

Sequences `N1` and `N2` implement a within-participant matched crossover. Within
each phase a participant solves both variants of each topology pair, one by the
competent direct route and one by the sparse strip, separated by the other pair.
Across sequences each variant is assigned once to each route. Direct work is
specified as the shortest competent nodal solution; independent reviewers must
reject a baseline that forces avoidable work.

## Observable and analysis contract

The TSV loader restores phase, pair, variant, route, and strip visibility from the
frozen schedule. It restores correctness from the exact symbolic answer rather
than trusting an entered Boolean. It also refuses invalid delays, reused phase
sessions, inconsistent reliable phase sums, mixed participant sequences, duplicate
slots, and undeclared error codes.

A pair contributes timing only when both attempts are complete, exactly correct,
and route-adherent. For participant `p`, phase `q`, and the two topology families,

```text
d[p,q,f] = t_sparse - t_direct
z[p,q]   = (d[p,q,1] + d[p,q,2]) / 2.
```

Only `z[p,q]` is an independent timing unit. A phase mean and sample variance are
computed across participants; variance remains absent with fewer than two complete
clusters. Accuracy counts retain failed attempts. Even when synthetic fixtures
make every design input computable, the constructor fixes speed, retention, and
transfer dispositions to `not_evaluated`.

## Independent key preflight

Each variant requires two qualified reviewers who independently solved it and were
blind to authored routes and peer reviews. They separately judge the exact answer,
both route keys, and direct-baseline competence. One admitted negative judgment has
precedence and yields `revision_required`; insufficient admitted positive reviews
yields `additional_review_required`; only complete agreement yields
`ready_for_pilot`.

This preflight tests instrument validity, not framework benefit.

## Current evidence boundary

Both checked-in TSVs contain headers and zero rows. The executable audit therefore
returns:

```text
exact keys:       12 constructed
key preflight:    additional_review_required
learner data:     unobserved
speed:            not_evaluated
retention:        not_evaluated
transfer:         not_evaluated
```

The stage closes the acquisition-capability gap and nothing beyond it. It does not
measure unprompted recognition of when to refuse sparse processing, and it does not
freeze a confirmatory effect threshold, accuracy tolerance, sample size,
uncertainty rule, or stopping rule. Actual independent reviews must precede a
pilot; a later confirmatory contract must then be evaluated on new held-out
participants.
