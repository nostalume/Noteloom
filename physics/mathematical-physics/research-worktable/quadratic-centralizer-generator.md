# Quadratic centralizer generator for natural Hamiltonians

## 1. Capability and bounded horizon

The filtered-centralizer calculus still leaves a hard choice: which symbol
covariance modules should be searched?  For a natural Hamiltonian

```text
h(q,p)=1/2 g^ij(q)p_i p_j+V(q),                            (1)
```

this node removes that choice for time-reversal-even integrals of momentum degree
at most two on a constant-curvature carrier.  The metric generates the complete
principal-symbol module; the potential then selects its compatible subspace.

The executable horizon is narrower: the Euclidean plane, rational polynomial
`V(x,y)`, and scalar quadratic integrals.  Higher dimensions, nonconstant
curvature, rational/singular potentials, magnetic fields, and spin bundles remain
outside the tool even though the invariant construction indicates their entry
points.

## 2. Metric-to-representation construction

The kinetic symbol `T=1/2 g^ij p_i p_j` first constructs its Killing-vector Lie
algebra.  On the Euclidean plane the three moment maps are

```text
P_x=p_x,             P_y=p_y,
J=x p_y-y p_x.                                           (2)
```

Their symmetric products generate a six-dimensional rank-two Killing-tensor
module.  The constant-curvature generation theorem is the completeness contract:
no additional quadratic geodesic symbol lies outside this module.  Equivalently,
solving the Killing equation directly gives

```text
K^xx = a y^2+2b y+c,
K^yy = a x^2+2d x+e,
K^xy =-a xy-bx-dy+f.                                    (3)
```

Substitution into `partial^(i K^{jk)}=0` cancels every cubic-momentum coefficient.
Thus (3) is not an arbitrary degree ansatz: it is the complete representation
module generated by `Sym^2(e(2))` modulo its algebraic redundancies.

## 3. The potential constructs the compatible kernel

Seek

```text
q_K=1/2 K^ij p_i p_j+W(q).                               (4)
```

Evaluate `{h,q_K}` on the same phase-space point.  The cubic part vanishes by the
Killing equation.  The remaining linear part is

```text
{h,q_K} = (K dV-dW)^j p_j.                               (5)
```

Consequently `W` exists locally exactly when the one-form `K dV` is closed:

```text
B_V(K)=d(K dV)=0.                                        (6)
```

`B_V` is the Bertrand--Darboux compatibility map.  It is linear in the six
parameters of (3), although it may be nonlinear in the supplied coefficients of
`V`.  Therefore the complete quadratic-symbol search is one kernel computation:

```text
Z_2(h;V) = ker(B_V) subset K_2(E^2).                     (7)
```

The metric tensor always lies in the kernel and reconstructs `h`; quotienting its
line removes that trivial symmetry.  Additive constants give the scalar identity
operator and are also reported as a universal trivial direction rather than a
hidden generator.  A zero quotient is a relative nonexistence theorem for
nonconstant scalar even quadratic integrals, not merely “nothing was guessed.”

## 4. Quantum lift as an executable equality

For every compatible pair `(K,W)`, use divergence ordering

```text
H = -1/2 Delta+V,
Q_K = -1/2 partial_i K^ij partial_j+W.                    (8)
```

On flat Cartesian space the tool composes these differential operators with the
full Leibniz rule and checks

```text
[H,Q_K]=0                                                (9)
```

coefficient by coefficient over the exact polynomial ring.  This is stronger than
checking only the classical symbol.  It is still a formal equality on the
polynomial/smooth core; self-adjoint closure, boundary conditions, and strong
commutation are analytic obligations.

## 5. Returned object and refusal

```text
QuadraticCentralizer(
  metric Killing algebra,
  complete rank-two module basis,
  monomial-labelled compatibility matrix B_V,
  kernel basis with H distinguished,
  reconstructed W for each generator,
  quantum differential operators,
  exact commutator certificates,
  hidden quotient dimension,
  analytic and global-group obligations
).
```

Possible results are:

- `NontrivialQuadraticCentralizer` when `dim ker(B_V)>1`;
- `MetricOnly` when the Hamiltonian line is the entire kernel;
- `Refused` when coefficients lie outside the admitted exact domain or a claimed
  closed form fails reconstruction.

The output is an operator algebra, not automatically a Lie group.  Two commuting
self-adjoint generators lead to joint spectral decomposition; several quadratic
generators may close only as a polynomial algebra because their commutator can
have order three.

Both statuses are understood modulo the scalar identity operator.

## 6. Human computability

The human proof retains only:

```text
three Killing generators
 -> their six-dimensional symmetric square
 -> one linear compatibility map B_V
 -> kernel modulo the metric
 -> finite operator presentation.                       (10)
```

All coordinate coefficient rows live in the replay certificate.  Named coordinate
systems and special functions occur only after a compatible `K` is interpreted.
When `K` has simple normal eigenvectors, the associated eigendistributions can
construct separation coordinates; this implication uses the stated separability
theorem contract and is not inferred from a matrix diagonalization alone.

## 7. Completeness and generalization boundary

Completeness has three stacked reasons:

1. the constant-curvature theorem generates every rank-two Killing tensor from
   Killing vectors;
2. equation (6) is necessary and sufficient for the scalar lower term `W` locally;
3. exact operator composition checks the declared flat quantum lift.

The persistent limiting check also exposes symmetry enhancement without a group
label: the anisotropic quadratic potential has kernel dimension `2`, the isotropic
limit has dimension `4`, and the free limit recovers all `6` Killing tensors.

Remove constant curvature and item 1 may fail.  Add first-order momentum terms,
magnetic curvature, matrix potentials, or spin and the module and compatibility
map change.  Raise the order and `Sym^m(g)` may contain redundancies or fail to
generate all Killing tensors outside constant curvature.  These are typed re-entry
conditions, not invitations to enlarge a coordinate ansatz indefinitely.

The [Euclidean-plane transfer](benchmarks/quadratic-centralizer-e2.md) exercises the
same generator on a hidden rotated-separable quartic and a generic negative
control.  The [radial extension](radial-centralizer-extension.md) now admits
Coulomb coefficients and selects its Runge--Lenz tensors through (6), not by hand.
The remaining dimensional transfer is the complete rank-two module on `E^3`.
