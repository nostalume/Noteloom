# Boundary-compatible representation reduction and complexity

## 1. Spine binding and horizon

- **Upstream anchor:** the group-free operator inverse and whole-route complexity
  audit, corrected by the observation that a boundary-value problem is not merely
  a differential expression.
- **Bridge question:** can the boundary-compatible group be constructed from the
  operator/domain pair, and does its representation decomposition reduce a named
  spectral computation rather than only reorganize it?
- **Invariant target:** all Ritz eigenvalues, with multiplicities, of the
  Dirichlet Laplacian on the same finite polynomial space.
- **Baseline:** one dense generalized eigenproblem in a Cartesian polynomial
  presentation.
- **Competing route:** discover the connected domain stabilizer, decompose the
  same space into its characters, and solve the resulting exact block pencils.
- **Special resources:** a centered quadratic boundary, the unit disk positive
  case, exact rational form integrals, and dense all-eigenpair cost proxies.
- **Control:** a noncircular ellipse has the same differential expression but no
  connected boundary-compatible Euclidean generator.
- **Stop condition:** exact pencil equality and a complete discovery-to-recovery
  cost comparison. Continuum convergence and sparse/partial eigensolvers remain
  separate nodes.

Sources [42--44](../sources.md) supply the boundary-symmetry and generalized-
eigenproblem theorem/substrate contracts. The stabilizer, exact forms, and cost
counts below are constructed internally.

## 2. The domain is part of the operator

Let

```text
Omega_G={x in R^2 : rho_G(x)=x^T G x-1<0},
H_G=(-Delta,H^1_0(Omega_G)),                            (1)
```

where `G` is symmetric positive definite. The differential expression `-Delta`
has the three-dimensional connected Euclidean Killing algebra generated by
translations and rotation. A formal commutator is insufficient: the flow must
also preserve `Omega_G` and its Dirichlet trace subspace.

Write an affine Euclidean Killing field as

```text
X(x)=a+omega Jx,       J=[[0,-1],[1,0]].                (2)
```

Tangency is generated rather than assumed:

```text
X rho_G
 =2 x^T G a + omega x^T(GJ-JG)x.                       (3)
```

Because `G` is invertible, (3) vanishes on the boundary only if `a=0`; rotation
survives exactly when `GJ=JG`. In two dimensions this means `G=cI`. Therefore

```text
Lie(H_expression)=e(2),
Lie(H_disk domain)=so(2),
Lie(H_noncircular ellipse domain)=0.                   (4)
```

This is the bilateral inverse step: the group is the output of the joint
coefficient-and-boundary stabilizer. Polar variables are not an input; the
rotation flow constructed by (3) subsequently generates its character label.

## 3. Same-space representation decomposition

For the unit disk choose the Dirichlet-conforming space

```text
V_p=(1-r^2) P_p(C),
D=dim V_p=(p+1)(p+2)/2.                                (5)
```

The Cartesian presentation `(1-x^2-y^2)x^a y^b` and the complex presentation
`(1-r^2)z^a zbar^b` span exactly the same `V_p`, because
`z=x+iy,zbar=x-iy` is invertible with determinant `-2i`. Rotation assigns weight
`m=a-b`. For one representative of `|m|`, use

```text
phi_(m,j)=(1-r^2) z^m (z zbar)^j,
d_m=floor((p-m)/2)+1,       m=0,...,p.                 (6)
```

The `m=0` block occurs once and every `m>0` block twice. Direct summation gives

```text
d_0+2 sum_(m=1)^p d_m=D.                               (7)
```

No Cartesian monomial expansion is needed. Character orthogonality makes both
forms vanish between unequal weights. Within one block, with `q=m+j+k`, exact
radial integration gives

```text
M^(m)_(jk)/pi = 2/[(q+1)(q+2)(q+3)],                  (8)

K^(m)_(jk)/pi
 =4[(m+k+1)(k+1)/((q+1)(q+2))
    -(m+k)k/(q(q+1))],                                 (9)
```

where the second term is zero when its numerator is zero. The executable checks
Hermiticity and positive definiteness exactly over the rationals. Since (5) is
the same space and (8)--(9) are the same mass and Dirichlet forms, the full pencil
is congruent to their direct sum. Hence its complete Ritz spectrum is the union
of the block spectra, with the multiplicities in (7).

## 4. Whole-route computational complexity

The cost model deliberately names its backend: dense symmetric-definite
all-eigenpair solution on one fixed Ritz space. It is not a claim about every
finite-element or iterative solver.

| Cost | Cartesian full pencil | Constructed representation route |
| --- | --- | --- |
| symmetry discovery | none, but no structure learned | fixed `3`-generator tangency system in this 2D grammar |
| basis labeling | implicit `D` monomials | `D` pairs `(m,j)` |
| pencil assembly/storage | `Theta(D^2)=Theta(p^4)` | `sum_m Theta(d_m^2)=Theta(p^3)` |
| dense eigensolve work | `Theta(D^3)=Theta(p^6)` | `sum_m Theta(d_m^3)=Theta(p^4)` |
| ideal block-parallel critical path | `Theta(p^6)` | `max_m Theta(d_m^3)=Theta(p^3)` |
| spectral recovery | `D` labels | `D` labels with `m>0` multiplicity two |

The reduction is not inferred from big-O notation. For `p=2s`, the executable
counts

```text
sum_m d_m^3=(s+1)^3+s^2(s+1)^2/2,
sum_m d_m^2=(s+1)^2+s(s+1)(2s+1)/3.                  (10)
```

Two exact checkpoints are:

| `p` | `D` | full `D^3` | `sum d_m^3` | full `D^2` | `sum d_m^2` | `max d_m^3` |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 6 | 28 | 21,952 | 136 | 784 | 44 | 64 |
| 32 | 561 | 176,558,481 | 41,905 | 314,721 | 3,281 | 4,913 |

At `p=6`, including exact form assembly, stabilizer discovery, label generation,
and recovery changes the work proxy from `22,792` to `261`; the reduced/full
ratio is `261/22792`. The gain is therefore not obtained by ignoring construction
cost. For repeated spectral queries on the same domain, the stabilizer and sector
labels are additionally reusable.

## 5. Meaning and failure boundary

This benchmark reverses the earlier Cartesian-factor preference under a different
physical contract. With a disk boundary, Cartesian factors do not preserve the
domain, while the constructed rotation representation does. The result is both
human compression—one weight and one radial index—and a strict reduction in this
dense finite-dimensional computation.

The ellipse control is equally important. The machine returns
`NoContinuousBoundarySymmetry`; it does not attach the expression-level `E(2)`
group to the boundary problem. Discrete reflection sectors, elliptic-coordinate
separation, boundary-integral methods, and approximate symmetries may still be
useful, but they are different constructors and costs.

Not certified: convergence of these Ritz values to the continuum spectrum;
conditioning improvement of the monomial radial basis; dense exponents for sparse,
iterative, or partial-spectrum solvers; arbitrary boundary grammars; or useful
continuous representation reduction on a generic domain.
