# Group-free inverse for a quadratic Schrödinger PDE

## Capability

Construct raising/lowering operators, spectrum, and the effective dynamical Lie
algebra directly from the coefficients of a common multidimensional second-order
PDE.  No rotation, Heisenberg, or metaplectic group is supplied as input.

## Typed input

On `R^n`, let

```text
H = -nabla_x^T A nabla_x + x^T K x,                    (1)
```

where `A` and `K` are real symmetric positive-definite matrices.  The core is the
Schwartz space.  The observable is the discrete spectrum generated from the
normalizable lowest-weight state.

Positivity is a special resource: it supplies real square roots, positive mode
frequencies, and a normalizable Gaussian.  Indefinite quadratic Hamiltonians
belong to a different real-form and spectral problem.

## 1. Construct the modes from the PDE coefficients

The principal symbol constructs the kinetic matrix `A`.  Set

```text
x = A^(1/2) y,
Omega^2 = A^(1/2) K A^(1/2).                            (2)
```

Both sides of (2) are constructed from the supplied coefficients.  Direct
substitution gives

```text
nabla_x = A^(-1/2) nabla_y,
-nabla_x^T A nabla_x = -nabla_y^T nabla_y,
x^T K x = y^T Omega^2 y.                               (3)
```

By positivity, the finite-dimensional spectral theorem constructs an orthogonal
`O` and positive frequencies `omega_i` such that

```text
O^T Omega^2 O = diag(omega_1^2,...,omega_n^2).
```

With `z=O^T y`, equations (1)--(3) produce

```text
H = sum_i (-partial_i^2 + omega_i^2 z_i^2).             (4)
```

This is an invariant generalized-eigenvalue construction; individual PDE
components are not searched for raising operators.

## 2. Residual-forced first-order factors

For each eigendirection of `Omega^2`, factor its quadratic principal symbol and
choose mutually adjoint first-order candidates

```text
a_i^- = partial_i + omega_i z_i,
a_i^+ = -partial_i + omega_i z_i.                       (5)
```

Composition on a Schwartz function computes

```text
a_i^+ a_i^-
  = -partial_i^2 + omega_i^2 z_i^2 - omega_i,

a_i^- a_i^+
  = -partial_i^2 + omega_i^2 z_i^2 + omega_i.           (6)
```

The zero-order correction `omega_i` is forced by the derivative of the linear
coefficient in (5).  Summing (6) reconstructs the supplied PDE:

```text
H = sum_i a_i^+ a_i^- + sum_i omega_i.                  (7)
```

Thus factorization is obtained from matrix square root and diagonalization, not
from a previously known wavefunction.

## 3. Construct the operator Lie algebra

Computing both compositions in (6), and using independence of the constructed
modes, gives

```text
[a_i^-,a_j^+] = 2 omega_i delta_ij I,
[a_i^-,a_j^-] = [a_i^+,a_j^+] = 0,
[H,a_i^+] =  2 omega_i a_i^+,
[H,a_i^-] = -2 omega_i a_i^-.                           (8)
```

The span of `{I,a_i^-,a_i^+,H}` closes with constant structure constants.  It is
the oscillator Lie algebra: the Heisenberg algebra constructed by the first three
relations, extended by the derivation generated by `H`.  A corresponding group is
integrated only after choosing the real/skew-adjoint convention.

## 4. Construct the representation and spectrum

The first-order equations `a_i^- psi_0=0` are compatible because the lowering
operators commute.  Integration gives, up to normalization,

```text
psi_0(z) = exp[-(1/2) sum_i omega_i z_i^2].              (9)
```

Positivity makes (9) square integrable.  Equations (7) and (8) then give

```text
E_0 = sum_i omega_i,
psi_(n_1,...,n_n) proportional to
  product_i (a_i^+)^(n_i) psi_0,
E_(n_1,...,n_n) = sum_i (2n_i+1)omega_i.                (10)
```

Completeness is imported from the standard one-dimensional Hermite spectral
theorem after the unitary linear change of variables; it is not inferred merely
from formal ladder closure.

As an independent local check, the two identities in (6) and all commutators in
(8) were evaluated with exact integer polynomial arithmetic on monomials of
degrees zero through eight.  Every residual vanished.  This finite check guards
the displayed signs; the operator calculation in (6)--(8), not the sample, is the
all-core argument.

## 5. The inverse does not return one predetermined group

For arbitrary unequal frequencies, (8) constructs the Heisenberg/oscillator
dynamical algebra.  If all frequencies coincide, the bilinears
`a_i^+a_j^-` acquire a larger unitary degeneracy algebra.  Rational frequency
relations can generate higher polynomial conserved operators.  Therefore the PDE
itself decides which enhancement occurs:

```text
generic frequencies -> ladder algebra with little degeneracy,
equal frequencies   -> additional compact U(n)-type commutant,
resonant frequencies -> possible higher polynomial symmetry.
```

This is why the inverse should return a tagged family of effective symmetry and
dynamical algebras rather than “the group of the PDE.”

## 6. Human and machine computability

The entire construction requires:

1. one positive matrix square root;
2. one finite-dimensional spectral decomposition of `Omega^2`;
3. `n` factor pairs and their universal commutator rule;
4. one cyclic lowest-weight construction.

It replaces a multidimensional component eigenfunction calculation by semantic
linear algebra plus reusable operators.  A computer may execute the matrix
operations, but a human can reproduce the route without enumerating basis states.

## 7. Failure certificates

Return a different result when:

- `A` or `K` is singular: constrained or flat directions require quotienting or
  continuous-spectrum analysis;
- `Omega^2` has negative eigenvalues: the Gaussian is not normalizable and the
  chosen lowest-weight unitary representation fails;
- boundary conditions are not preserved by `a_i^+` and `a_i^-`;
- linear terms are present but the required affine translation is not admitted;
- magnetic position--momentum cross terms are present: a symplectic, rather than
  orthogonal, normal form is required.

The last case is the natural re-entry point for the metaplectic/symplectic
extension; it is not repaired by pretending (2) still applies.
