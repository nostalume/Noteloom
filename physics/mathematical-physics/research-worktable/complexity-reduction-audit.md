# Whole-route complexity audit

## 1. The observable fixes what may be compared

“Algebra is simpler” is meaningful only after two routes compute the same named
observable to the same tolerance and recovery convention.  For a route `R`, use

```text
C_R(O) = (
  discover, construct, lift, close, decompose,
  analytic, solve, recover, certify, human_depth
).                                                       (1)
```

These are typed costs, not automatically commensurate numbers.  Examples include
the number and order of determining equations, expression growth, number of
representation labels, asymptotic arithmetic work, conditioning, theorem debt,
and semantic transformations a human must hold at once.

Validity precedes cost: a route with no domain or error certificate cannot defeat
a valid baseline merely because its formal core is short.

## 2. Stage verdicts

Each stage receives one of four verdicts:

```text
Eliminated     the competing work is no longer required for O;
Compressed     a generative object replaces a growing family of instances;
Relocated      comparable difficulty reappears in another representation;
Unpaid         a necessary proof or computation is absent.
```

The central danger is `Relocated`: solving a Riccati equation obtained by inserting
an already known ground state, or solving centralizer determining equations as
large as the original eigenproblem, changes notation but not computability.

## 3. One-time and per-query cost

Representation machinery often pays a larger construction cost once and a small
cost for many sectors.  Report

```text
C_total(N;O_1,...,O_N) = C_once + sum_(a=1)^N C_query(O_a).          (2)
```

The break-even query count is part of the result.  A symmetry algebra can be the
right family-level machine and the wrong tool for one local wavefunction.  A
cyclic reduction can be optimal for one correlator and useless for broad reuse.

## 4. Human computability

Human compression is not line count.  Count the independent semantic objects and
nonlocal transformations needed to regenerate the answer.  Prefer:

- one covariance type over all tensor components;
- one finite presentation over tables of matrix elements;
- one Casimir evaluation over a radial equation for every angular label;
- one recurrence or branching rule over a catalogue of special polynomials;
- one obstruction class over an inconclusive failed search.

Component calculations may remain in the replay certificate.  The retained proof
spine must explain why the construction works and where it stops.

## 5. Comparison rule

Two valid routes are compared by Pareto dominance unless the user supplies
weights.  A claimed gain must name:

1. the common observable and accuracy;
2. the baseline route;
3. the stages eliminated or compressed;
4. the stages relocated or unpaid;
5. one-time versus repeated-query behavior;
6. what is not recovered, such as coordinate wavefunctions or scattering data.

The [Coulomb audit](benchmarks/coulomb-hidden-so4.md#9-whole-route-complexity-audit)
applies this rule to bound-state energies and degeneracies.  The algebraic route
compresses all angular sectors into two `su(2)` labels and one Casimir identity,
but it does not eliminate the analytic existence/completeness proof or reconstruct
radial wavefunctions.

## 12. Boundary cost can reverse the preferred decomposition

The [boundary-domain probe](benchmarks/boundary-domain-symmetry-complexity.md)
compares the complete Ritz spectrum in exactly the same polynomial space. A
Cartesian tensor split is not admissible because its factors do not preserve the
disk boundary. The machine instead intersects the three expression-level `e(2)`
generators with the boundary stabilizer, retaining only `so(2)`.

For polynomial degree `p`, `D=(p+1)(p+2)/2` Cartesian coefficients are replaced by
blocks `d_m=floor((p-m)/2)+1`. Including stabilizer discovery, weight labeling,
exact form assembly, dense block solution, and multiplicity recovery gives:

| Quantity | Unreduced | Boundary-compatible representation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| assembly | `Theta(p^4)` | `Theta(p^3)` |
| dense all-spectrum solve | `Theta(p^6)` | `Theta(p^4)` total, `Theta(p^3)` ideal parallel critical path |
| storage | `Theta(p^4)` | `Theta(p^3)` |
| recovery | `Theta(p^2)` | `Theta(p^2)` |

At `p=6`, the executable whole-route proxy is `22792 -> 261`; at `p=32`,
the solve proxy is `176558481 -> 41905`. These are exact algebraic counts, not
timings. The verdict is `RepresentationReduction` only for the stated dense
all-eigenpair backend. Continuum error, monomial conditioning, sparse iteration,
and partial-spectrum targets are explicitly unpaid, so the asymptotic gain is not
silently generalized to every numerical method.

## 13. Fixed accuracy exposes the actual geometric cost parameter

The [fixed-accuracy boundary comparison](benchmarks/boundary-fixed-accuracy-comparison.md)
now pays part of the debt left by the dense Ritz result. It computes the same
first six continuum Dirichlet-disk eigenvalues with multiplicity, checks both
routes against the same `2/25` maximum relative-error target, and compares a
rotation-sector finite-volume route with a sparse Cartesian shift-invert route.

| Quantity at first passing resolution | Representation route | Cartesian sparse baseline |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| resolution | 16 radial cells in each of six constructed `|m|` sectors | 24 intervals across the diameter |
| active unknowns | 96 | 437 |
| assembled nonzeros | 276 | 2,093 |
| maximum continuum relative error | `2.98e-3` | `4.30e-2` |
| maximum algebraic residual | below `3.6e-12` | below `2.6e-14` |

The failed 16-interval Cartesian refinement remains charged. Setup, solution, and
recovery times are recorded separately, but one-machine wall time is classified
as an observation rather than an asymptotic claim. The supported verdict is
`RepresentationReductionAtTolerance` against this declared baseline.

The reduction comes from cohomogeneity, not dense-matrix cubic arithmetic. For an
`n`-dimensional domain with generic orbit dimension `d`, the sector PDE lives on a
quotient of dimension `q=n-d`. At spatial scale `h`, the comparison grammar is

```text
N_full=Theta(h^(-n)),
N_reduced=sum_(pi in F_Lambda) b_pi Theta(h^(-q)),
```

where `F_Lambda` must be generated without an unbounded irrep scan. On the disk,
`lambda>=m^2` supplies a branch-and-bound certificate: after `|m|=5`, the first
unseen lower bound is `36`, above the computed sixth eigenvalue `30.5573`.
For a more general compact action, a problem-specific estimate such as
`lambda_min(pi)>=a C_2(pi)+b` can play the same role.

This closes only the simple-grid, moderate-accuracy disk comparison. A priori
continuum error, boundary-fitted finite elements or spectral elements,
preconditioning, high-accuracy convergence, and variable-coefficient quotient
PDEs remain `Unpaid`. The next complexity test is therefore a nonseparable axial
three-dimensional problem, where symmetry reduces a 3D PDE to finitely many 2D
PDE sectors rather than to special-function ODEs.

## 14. Source support converts cohomogeneity into a PDE-level gain

The [axial quotient-PDE benchmark](benchmarks/axial-nonseparable-quotient-pde.md)
executes that next test. Its scalar divergence-form coefficients depend jointly on
`r` and `z`, so axial character reduction leaves coupled 2D PDEs. The exact
manufactured forcing has three real character modes: `(0,cos)`, `(1,cos)`, and
`(2,sin)`.

With 24 angular nodes and `N_q=N_r N_z` quotient cells, the carrier comparison is

```text
N_full=24 N_q,
N_reduced=3 N_q.                                               (14.1)
```

At a weighted-relative-`L^2` target of `1/50`, both routes fail at `6x6` quotient
cells and pass at `8x8`:

| Accepted-resolution quantity | Full 3D sparse route | Three 2D sector PDEs |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| solved unknowns | 1,536 | 192 |
| assembled nonzeros | 9,984 | 864 |
| continuum relative error | `1.220e-2` | `1.220e-2` |
| full/reconstructed discrepancy | — | below `6.3e-16` |

The full matrix is constructed as
`A_Q tensor I+diag(c/r^2) tensor L_theta`; character analysis produces exactly
`A_m=A_Q+mu_m diag(c/r^2)`. Hence the gain preserves the same discrete problem,
not merely the same approximate continuum answer.

The interpretation is capability-specific. For the source solve, equivariance
guarantees that the solution has the forcing's finite representation support. A
full resolvent, arbitrary forcing, or nonlinear term can populate infinitely many
sectors and cannot reuse this finite-support verdict. Full-field synthesis also
retains `Theta(24 N_q)` output cost, although a sector-local observable avoids it.

This node closes the `3D PDE -> 2D PDE family` bridge for a product annular
boundary. Singular orbit strata, warped boundaries, spinor/tensor multiplicity
bundles, and iterative/preconditioned scaling remain `Unpaid`.

On the visible matrix-coefficient copy, the Casimir theorem contract gives

```text
T_X S(Y)=S((n+1)[a[X,Y]+b{X,Y}_0]),

```

For the same `SU(3)` preparation and observable, the PDE route again returns
`(5,1)` and retains both outer-multiplicity channels. The `SU(2)` transfer returns

| Cost | Component PDE presentation | Visible-core natural route |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| analytic fiber dimension | 8 | 8; no carrier-dimension gain |
| possible bilinear coefficients | 512 | two reduced couplings |
| differential action on the chosen preparation | solve/evaluate eight component equations | one Casimir scalar plus two matrix products |
| observable recovery | component contraction | one trace pairing |

This supports exact semantic and human compression on the declared core, but not
carrier-dimension reduction: both descriptions retain eight fiber degrees of
freedom. The spatial differential solve disappears only because the preparation is
an admitted Casimir eigenmodule. A runtime dominance claim remains unpaid because
no full spatial discretization was run. Full regular-representation multiplicities and the spectrum of the
non-invariant operator are also outside the bench. The strongest remaining cost is
now inverse discovery: the input still supplies `SU(n)` and the adjoint carrier.

## 20. The principal pairing generates the differential action

This adds construction cost rather than hiding it: exact Gram inversion, eight

The result is exact on `Sym(sl_n^*)` but has no positive Hilbert completion,

advances internal and human computability but not yet analytical computation. The

## 24. Generic planning exposes relocated orbit enumeration

The typed relation planner removes the G1 symmetry-name table. A rank, covariant
slot action, permutation generators, and relation certificate now generate the
structured route and its completeness witnesses. This is a real internal and human
gain: an anonymous tensor produces the same plan, while missing, ambiguous, and
uncertified relations have explicit refusals.

It does not improve the G1 defect calculation itself. More importantly, the generic
orbit constructor obtains 21 representatives by canonicalizing all 81 rank-four
indices under a group of order eight. The expanded planning audit is therefore:

| Proxy component | Raw route | Typed structured route |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| compiler upper/proxy | 1782 | 117 |
| target/orbit planning | 90 | 648 |
| expanded one-time total | 1872 | 765 |
| repeated-query break-even | 117 | 48 |

The raw break-even is shown only for scale; neither route would normally pay raw
stabilization once the structured certificate is admitted. The table also omits a
normalized metric-inversion count and is not a runtime measurement.

Thus abstraction has not hidden the enumeration, but it has relocated it. The next
gain must also separate certification from operation: the current benchmark
materializes raw and structured defect blocks to prove span equality, so 765 models
the selected route rather than the whole comparison process. Generate symmetric
blocks, exchanged blocks, exterior slots, and trace quotients in work proportional
to their retained coordinates; check symbolic budgets before materialization; and
make raw comparison an explicit audit. Only then should the planner be tested on a
genuinely different coupled carrier such as a spinor/Clifford covariance relation.
