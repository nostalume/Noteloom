# Coefficient-derived spectral-projector jet

## Frozen contract `C_G14_v1`

G4--G10 construct representation blocks from operator coefficients, but G11 begins
with a supplied moving projector. G14 closes that local inverse gap. Its inputs are
an exact Hermitian coefficient jet

```text
C, C_1=partial C, C_2=partial^2 C,                             (1)
```

a selected rational spectral value `lambda`, and a factorization budget. No
projector, eigenvector, transport generator, or group label is admitted as input.

## Base projector and gap

The G8 coefficient-algebra constructor obtains the minimal polynomial of `C`,
splits it over `Q`, and constructs spectral projectors polynomially. G14 selects
the sector labeled by `lambda`:

```text
C=sum_a lambda_a P_a,       P=P_lambda,
delta=min_{mu != lambda}|lambda-mu|.                              (2)
```

Multiplicity of `P` is retained. A scalar coefficient has no separated complement
and returns `SpectralGapClosed`; an absent label returns `SelectedClusterAbsent`.

## First derivative as a Sylvester solution

Differentiating `[C,P]=0` and `P^2=P` gives

```text
[C,P_1]+[C_1,P]=0,
P_1=P_1 P+P P_1.                                                   (3)
```

The second equality forces `P_1` off diagonal. Resolving the first across the
constructed complement sectors yields

```text
X_mu=P_mu C_1 P/(lambda-mu),
P_1=sum_mu (X_mu+X_mu^dagger).                                    (4)
```

Thus the Sylvester inverse is a finite projector operation, not an eigenvector
matching problem.

## Second derivative

Differentiate both laws again and define

```text
S=2[C_1,P_1]+[C_2,P].                                              (5)
```

The off-diagonal blocks solve the same separated Sylvester problem. The diagonal
blocks are forced by idempotency:

```text
Y_mu=P_mu S P/(lambda-mu),
P_2^off=sum_mu (Y_mu+Y_mu^dagger),
P_2^diag=-2 P P_1^2 P+2 Q P_1^2 Q,
P_2=P_2^off+P_2^diag.                                              (6)
```

The implementation then verifies exactly

```text
[C,P_2]+S=0,
P_2=P_2 P+P P_2+2P_1^2.                                          (7)
```

It also returns the invariant arrows `Q P_1 P`, `Q P_2 P` and the canonical
horizontal generator

```text
R_0=[P_1,P],              R_0^dagger=-R_0,
[R_0,P]=P_1.                                                        (8)
```

## Evidence and downstream use

For a two-cluster repeated-Pauli coefficient with spectrum `(2,5)`, G14 constructs
the rank-two selected projector, gap `3`, and twelve exact law certificates. When
the coefficient jet is generated by constant horizontal transport, it recovers
the original `P'`, `P''`, and transport generator exactly. The rigid adapter then
feeds G11 and G13 without receiving that generator as input. The resulting
two-momentum transition is

```text
0.07932539505161444 <= 4/45.                                      (9)
```

Rational carrier conjugation carries all three constructed projector jets by the
same conjugation and preserves the gap. A separate rank-two degenerate cluster in
dimension three remains one projector rather than two chosen eigenvectors. A
closed gap, absent spectral label, non-Hermitian derivative, and non-rigid request
for the narrower G11 adapter refuse distinctly.

## Cost, disposition, and boundary

The inherited G8 constructor owns minimal-polynomial and rational-factor search
cost. After its `s` spectral sectors are available, G14 performs `s-1` first and
`s-1` second cross-sector solves expressed as dense matrix products, hence
`O(s q^3)` arithmetic and `O(s q^2)` stored projector evidence. The witness records
carrier size, sector count, factor-candidate checks, and complement block count.

Disposition: **supported bounded** for finite Gaussian-rational Hermitian jets with
an isolated rational semisimple cluster. This is a genuine PDE-coefficient-to-
representation-bundle construction and supports internal cluster degeneracy.

General `P_2` need not equal `[[P_1,P],P_1]`; only that rigid subclass enters the
existing G11 constructor. Non-rigid projector jets, irrational or continuous
spectra, nonnormal coefficients, varying domains, crossings, global topology, and
nonlinear feedback remain outside this result.
