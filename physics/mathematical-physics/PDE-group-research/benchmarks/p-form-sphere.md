# Transfer bench: the de Rham complex on round spheres

## Spine binding

- **Upstream anchors:** the abstract matrix-coefficient calculus in
  [../representation-calculus.md](../nodes/representation-calculus.md), the scalar

- **Bridge question:** does the same interface survive a nontrivial `K`-type and

  `p`-forms.
- **Downstream effect:** tests whether the calculus extends beyond scalar

## Horizon and conventions

Use the unit round sphere

```text
S^n = SO(n+1)/SO(n)
```

with `n>=2`, complex forms, positive Hodge Laplacian, integer level `ell>=1`, and
`0<=p<n/2`.  The remaining degrees follow from Hodge star; middle degree and the
two endpoint harmonic sectors are recorded separately.  This bounded range avoids
silently hiding even-dimensional middle-degree splitting.

## Branching output

At the base point the fiber is the nontrivial isotropy representation

```text
tau_p = exterior_power(p, C^n).
```

Frobenius reciprocity and the `SO(n+1) down to SO(n)` branching rule generate two
positive-spectrum towers in degree `p`:

```text
C_(ell,p): coexact p-forms, highest weight (ell,1^p),
E_(ell,p): exact p-forms,   highest weight (ell,1^(p-1)).
```

The exact tower is absent at `p=0`.  The notation `(ell,1^q)` uses orthogonal
highest-weight coordinates and is restricted to the stable range above.

## Casimir computation for the coexact tower

For `SO(N)` with `N=n+1`, the quadratic Casimir in these coordinates is

```text
C_2(lambda) = sum_j lambda_j(lambda_j+N-2j).
```

Evaluate it on the generated weight `(ell,1^p)`.  The first coordinate contributes
`ell(ell+n-1)`, while the remaining `p` coordinates contribute

```text
sum_(j=2)^(p+1) (n+2-2j) = p(n-p-1).
```

Therefore

```text
lambda_co(ell,p)
  = ell(ell+n-1)+p(n-p-1)
  = (ell+p)(ell+n-p-1).
```

This value is produced by the highest weight; no radial or tensor-component PDE is
solved.

## The differential arrow generates the exact tower

For a coexact `(p-1)`-form `alpha` with positive eigenvalue, the invariant identities
give

```text
Delta(d alpha)=d(Delta alpha),
delta d alpha=lambda_co(ell,p-1) alpha.
```

Thus `d alpha` is an exact `p`-eigenform in the same `SO(n+1)` representation, and

```text
lambda_ex(ell,p)
  = lambda_co(ell,p-1)
  = ell(ell+n-1)+(p-1)(n-p)
  = (ell+p-1)(ell+n-p).
```

Moreover,

```text
d/sqrt(lambda_ex(ell,p)): C_(ell,p-1) -> E_(ell,p)
```

is unitary, with inverse `delta/sqrt(lambda_ex(ell,p))`.  Hence the exact spectrum
is regenerated by one arrow rather than independently enumerated.

## Generated spectrum and heat trace

There are no harmonic `p`-forms on `S^n` for `0<p<n`.  In the declared range,

```text
Spec_positive(Delta_p)
  = {lambda_ex(ell,p)} union {lambda_co(ell,p)}, ell>=1,
```

with the exact term omitted for `p=0`.  If `V_lambda` denotes the corresponding
`SO(n+1)` irreducible, Weyl's dimension operation supplies its degeneracy.  Applying
the functional calculus to each generated block constructs the heat trace

```text
Tr exp(-t Delta_p)
  = b_p
    + sum_(ell>=1) dim V_(ell,1^(p-1)) exp(-t lambda_ex(ell,p))
    + sum_(ell>=1) dim V_(ell,1^p) exp(-t lambda_co(ell,p)),
```

where `b_p=1` for `p=0,n` and `0` otherwise, and inadmissible towers are omitted.
The heat trace, rather than a list of eigenforms, is the fixed observable recovered
from the representation decomposition.

## Concrete transfer: one-forms on `S^3`

For `n=3,p=1`, the two towers give

```text
lambda_ex(ell,1) = ell(ell+2),
lambda_co(ell,1) = (ell+1)^2.
```

Using `Spin(4) ~= SU(2)_L x SU(2)_R`, the exact tower has representation
`(ell/2,ell/2)` and dimension `(ell+1)^2`.  The coexact tower is the sum

```text
((ell+1)/2,(ell-1)/2) direct-sum
((ell-1)/2,(ell+1)/2),
```

of total dimension `2ell(ell+2)`.  Since `H^1(S^3)=0`, the positive heat trace is

```text
Tr exp(-t Delta_1)
  = sum_(ell>=1) [
      (ell+1)^2 exp(-t ell(ell+2))
      + 2ell(ell+2) exp(-t(ell+1)^2)
    ].
```

The first coexact level has eigenvalue `4` and dimension `6`, the Killing-vector
sector.  The entire vector-field observable has been reduced to two representation
towers and one exact/coexact arrow.

## Bilateral inverse test

From the graded PDE package `(d,delta,Delta)` one recovers

```text
d^2=delta^2=0,
{d,delta}=Delta,
[Delta,d]=[Delta,delta]=0,
```

and the exact/coexact pairing at every positive eigenvalue.  This reconstructs the
Hodge factorization algebra and its finite spectral complexes, but not `SO(n+1)`.

To recover an isometry-algebra candidate, the input must also retain first-order
operators or symbols.  Contractions `i_X` construct `L_X={d,i_X}`; closure of the
Cartan commutators can then recover a represented Lie bracket.  Completeness,
effectiveness, and topology are still required before returning a global group.

For the sphere, enrich the PDE package by the tangent rotation fields

```text
X_ij = x_i partial_j-x_j partial_i,  1<=i<j<=n+1,
L_ij = {d,i_(X_ij)}.
```

Their action on the same form core computes

```text
[L_ij,L_kl]
  = delta_jk L_il-delta_ik L_jl
    -delta_jl L_ik+delta_il L_jk.
```

The bounded Cartan inverse therefore returns the effective Lie algebra
`so(n+1)`.  Integrating these complete fields returns the effective rotation action;
the differential representation still does not distinguish all covering groups.
Removing the `L_ij` arrows from the input makes this recovery unavailable, which
is the required information-loss witness.

This is a decisive inverse obstruction: the scalar spectrum, and even the graded
Hodge relations, contain less information than the homogeneous-bundle
representation that generated them.

## Human-computability comparison

The coordinate route begins with `binomial(n,p)` local components, imposes
closed/co-closed constraints, solves coupled equations, and then counts independent
solutions.  The representation route retains:

```text
one isotropy type tau_p,
two highest-weight towers,
one Casimir evaluation,
one normalized d/delta arrow,
one Weyl-dimension operation.
```

This is a semantic reduction, not yet a measured computational-speed claim.  The
cost of branching and observable recovery must be included in any later comparison.

## Checks, status, and stop condition

- **Internal equalities:** expanded and factorized eigenvalues coincide, and
  `lambda_ex(ell,p)=lambda_co(ell,p-1)` exactly.
- **Theorem contracts:** compact Peter-Weyl decomposition, Frobenius reciprocity,
  orthogonal branching, Casimir/Hodge identification, Hodge decomposition, and
  Weyl dimension formula.
- **Regression boundary:** `p=0` recovers the scalar spherical eigenvalue
  `ell(ell+n-1)`.
- **Transfer witness:** `p=1` on `S^3` produces distinct exact and coexact towers
  with the heat-trace observable above.
- **Supported result:** the compact nontrivial-`K`-type forward compiler and the
  bounded inverse-information verdict pass this bench.
- **Stop:** further explicit sphere eigenforms do not advance the spine.
- **Re-entry:** pass to real hyperbolic `p`-forms only when the continuous
  Plancherel measure and possible middle-degree discrete sector are consumed by a
  fixed heat-kernel or resolvent comparison.
