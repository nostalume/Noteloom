# Source map

These sources are grouped by the semantic question they answer.  They are not
evidence for one universal notion of “the group of a differential equation.”

## Direct factorization and dynamical algebra

1. L. Infeld and T. E. Hull, “The Factorization Method,” *Reviews of Modern
   Physics* **23** (1951), 21–68.
   [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.23.21)

   - **Use here:** primary source for first-order differential-difference
     factorization of second-order eigenvalue problems with boundary conditions.
   - **Boundary:** a factorization table is not an inverse from an arbitrary
     equation to a unique Lie group.

2. F. Cooper, A. Khare, and U. Sukhatme, “Supersymmetry and Quantum Mechanics,”
   *Physics Reports* **251** (1995), 267–385.
   [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9405029)

   - **Use here:** partner Hamiltonians, intertwiners, shape invariance, isospectral
     families, and the relation between solvability and factorization.
   - **Boundary:** shape invariance is a strong special resource; it must not be
     assumed for a general Schrödinger operator.

3. A. V. Turbiner, “Lie-algebraic approach to the theory of polynomial solutions.
   I,” (1992).
   [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9209079)

   - **Use here:** a precise restricted inverse problem—recognize differential
     operators as polynomial elements of `U(sl₂)` in a projectivized
     representation preserving a polynomial subspace.
   - **Boundary:** this addresses exact/quasi-exact polynomial sectors, not all
     operators or their full analytic spectra.

## Differential equations to geometric symmetry

4. P. J. Olver, *Applications of Lie Groups to Differential Equations*, 2nd ed.,
   Graduate Texts in Mathematics 107, Springer.
   [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4612-4350-2)

   - **Use here:** prolonged group actions, determining equations, invariant
     solutions, generalized symmetries, and conservation laws.
   - **Boundary:** point/contact symmetry acts on equation geometry; an additional
     construction is required to obtain a unitary representation or spectral
     decomposition.

5. P. J. Olver, “Symmetry Groups and Group Invariant Solutions of Partial
   Differential Equations,” *Journal of Differential Geometry* **14** (1979),
   497–542. [author PDF](https://www-users.cse.umn.edu/~olver/s_/spde.pdf)

   - **Use here:** primary account of local transformation groups, jet-space
     formulation, and group-invariant PDE solutions.

## Representations to special functions and differential operators

6. R. A. Strichartz, *Special Functions and Linear Representations of Lie Groups*,
   CBMS Regional Conference Series 42, AMS, 1980.
   [AMS](https://bookstore.ams.org/CBMS/42)

   - **Use here:** `SU(2)`, `SO(3)`, `SL(2,R)`, spherical functions, Fourier/
     Plancherel transforms, and the realization of Jacobi and Legendre functions
     as representation-theoretic matrix coefficients.

7. NIST Digital Library of Mathematical Functions, §14.30, “Spherical and
   Spheroidal Harmonics.” [DLMF](https://dlmf.nist.gov/14.30)

   - **Use here:** authoritative normalization and differential identities for the
   first benchmark.  DLMF is a verification reference, not the generative origin
   of the group action.

8. N. Ja. Vilenkin, *Special Functions and the Theory of Group
   Representations*, Translations of Mathematical Monographs 22, AMS, 1968.
   [AMS](https://bookstore.ams.org/MMONO/22)

   - **Use here:** the general matrix-coefficient view in which differential
     equations, recurrences, addition formulas, and orthogonality are consequences
     of representation operations rather than independent special-function facts.

9. P. Etingof and A. Kirillov Jr., “A unified representation-theoretic approach
   to special functions” (1993).
   [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9312101)

   - **Use here:** matrix elements, characters, tensor-product coefficients, and
     intertwining operators as a generative source of classical and generalized
     special functions.

## Symmetric spaces and the active rank-one bench

10. S. Helgason, *Groups and Geometric Analysis: Integral Geometry, Invariant
    Differential Operators, and Spherical Functions*, Mathematical Surveys and
    Monographs 83, AMS, 2000. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1090/surv/083)

    - **Use here:** theorem contracts for polar integration, invariant differential
      operators, spherical functions, Fourier/Plancherel transforms, and
      eigenspace representations on `G/K`.

11. S. Helgason, *Geometric Analysis on Symmetric Spaces*, 2nd ed., Mathematical
    Surveys and Monographs 39, AMS, 2008.
    [AMS](https://bookstore.ams.org/view?ProductCode=SURV%2F39.R)

    - **Use here:** the relation among symmetric-space geometry, Lie-group
      representations, invariant differential equations, and eigenspace
      representations.

12. G. J. Heckman and E. M. Opdam, “Root systems and hypergeometric functions.
    I,” *Compositio Mathematica* **64** (1987), 329–352.
    [NUMDAM](https://www.numdam.org/item/CM_1987__64_3_329_0/)

    - **Use here:** the higher-rank transfer horizon, where restricted-root data
      generate a commuting algebra of differential operators and generalized
      hypergeometric functions.

13. P. Ganguly, R. Manna, and S. Thangavelu, “On a theorem of Chernoff on rank
    one Riemannian symmetric spaces,” *Journal of Functional Analysis* **282**
    (2022), 109351.
    [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfa.2021.109351)

    - **Use here:** a compact verification reference for the rank-one
      root-multiplicity/Jacobi parameter dictionary, radial spherical functions,
      and the Jacobi transform realization.

## Equations to monodromy and differential-Galois groups

14. J. Sauloy, *Differential Galois Theory through Riemann–Hilbert
   Correspondence*, Graduate Studies in Mathematics 177, AMS, 2016.
   [AMS](https://bookstore.ams.org/GSM/177)

   - **Use here:** local systems, monodromy, regular singular equations,
     Riemann–Hilbert correspondence, and differential-Galois groups.
   - **Boundary:** these groups act on analytic continuation or solution-field
     data; they are not automatically dynamical symmetry groups.

## Advanced categorical bridge

15. A. Beilinson and J. Bernstein, “Localisation de g-modules,” *C. R. Acad. Sci.
   Paris* **292** (1981), 15–18.
   [bibliographic record](https://cris.tau.ac.il/en/publications/localisation-de-g-modules/)

   - **Use here:** a theorem-level route between suitable Lie-algebra modules and
     twisted D-modules on flag varieties.
   - **Boundary:** hypotheses such as reductivity, infinitesimal character,
     twisting, and exactness/localization conditions must be stated.  It is not the
     first implementation target.

## Homogeneous bundles and differential-form spectra

16. A. Ikeda and Y. Taniguchi, “Spectra and eigenforms of the Laplacian on
    `S^n` and `P^n(C)`,” *Osaka Journal of Mathematics* **15** (1978), 515–546.
    [open-access version of record](https://doi.org/10.18910/6956)

    - **Use here:** primary source for realizing differential forms as sections of
      homogeneous bundles, computing multiplicities by Frobenius reciprocity,
      identifying the Hodge Laplacian with the group Casimir on compact symmetric
      spaces, and determining the sphere spectrum by orthogonal branching.
    - **Boundary:** metric normalization and middle-degree representation splitting
      must be carried explicitly.

17. G. B. Folland, “Harmonic analysis of the de Rham complex on the sphere,”
    *Journal für die reine und angewandte Mathematik* **398** (1989), 130–143.
    [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1515/crll.1989.398.130)

    - **Use here:** the de Rham complex itself as the harmonic-analysis object,
      including irreducible subspaces and the actions of `d`, `d*`, and the
      Laplacian.

18. R. Camporesi and A. Higuchi, “The Plancherel measure for `p`-forms in real
    hyperbolic spaces,” *Journal of Geometry and Physics* **15** (1994), 57–94.
    [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/0393-0440(94)90047-7)

    - **Use here:** noncompact transfer contract for the continuous `p`-form
      Plancherel measure, sphere/hyperbolic spectral relation, heat kernels, and
      the even-dimensional middle-degree discrete sector.
   - **Boundary:** these analytic data cannot be inferred by merely replacing
     circular functions with hyperbolic functions in the compact formulas.

## Tensor operators and filtered differential operators

19. A. U. Klimyk, “Wigner--Eckart theorem for locally compact groups,”
    *Theoretical and Mathematical Physics* **8** (1971), 668--672.
    [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01038675)

    - **Use here:** primary theorem contract for treating an operator family that
      transforms irreducibly as an intertwiner and separating group coupling from
      reduced operator data.
    - **Boundary:** the compact, completely reducible case is the active
      constructor.  Outer tensor-product multiplicities must be retained rather
      than compressed into a single reduced matrix element.

20. A. Deitmar, “Invariant operators on higher `K`-types,” *Journal für die reine
    und angewandte Mathematik* **412** (1990), 97--107.
    [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1515/crll.1990.412.97)

    - **Use here:** higher-`K`-type and universal-enveloping-algebra realization of
      invariant differential operators, which is the trivial operator-type
      boundary of the new compiler.
    - **Boundary:** invariant operators alone yield block diagonalization and do
      not decompose symmetry-breaking operator types.

21. C. Duval, P. Lecomte, and V. Ovsienko, “Conformally equivariant
    quantization: existence and uniqueness,” *Annales de l'Institut Fourier*
    **49** (1999), 1999--2029.
    [version of record](https://www.numdam.org/articles/10.5802/aif.1744/)

    - **Use here:** a precise example in which a symbol-to-differential-operator
      splitting is itself equivariant and canonical under additional geometric
      hypotheses.
    - **Boundary:** the present compact-group constructor needs only an averaged
      invariant connection and records its noncanonical lower-order splitting.
      It does not import the stronger conformal existence/uniqueness claim.

22. G. Sellaroli, “Non-compact groups, tensor operators and applications to
    quantum gravity” (2016). [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.07795)

    - **Use here:** basis-free tensor-operator intertwiners, tensor-product
      multiplicities, and a documented noncompact extension horizon.
    - **Boundary:** noncompact decompositions may fail complete reducibility or
      require continuous spectra; they do not inherit compact character
      projectors.

23. R. Slansky, “Group theory for unified model building,” *Physics Reports*
    **79** (1981), 1--128.
    [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/0370-1573(81)90092-2)

    - **Use here:** tabulated compact-group tensor and branching data, including
      the `SU(3)` adjoint product used as the outer-multiplicity witness.
    - **Boundary:** a tensor-product table supplies the allowed intertwiner
      dimension, not the reduced differential operator or its analytic domain.

## Coordinate-free PDE and spinor factorization

24. D. J. Saunders, *The Geometry of Jet Bundles*, London Mathematical Society
    Lecture Note Series 142, Cambridge University Press, 1989.
    [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511526411)

    - **Use here:** jet bundles as the coordinate-independent carrier of finite
      derivative data and the universal geometric setting for differential
      operators.
    - **Boundary:** a jet presentation makes coordinate covariance explicit but
      does not itself construct a spectral or representation decomposition.

25. T. Friedrich, *Dirac Operators in Riemannian Geometry*, Graduate Studies in
    Mathematics 25, AMS, 2000.
    [AMS](https://bookstore.ams.org/GSM/25)

    - **Use here:** Clifford algebras, spin and `Spin^c` structures, spinor
      bundles, Dirac operators, self-adjointness, and the geometry behind
      Dirac/Laplace factorization.
    - **Boundary:** existence of a scalar metric symbol does not by itself supply
      a global spin structure or a compatible Dirac square root.

26. B. Thaller, *The Dirac Equation*, Springer, 1992.
    [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-662-02753-0)

    - **Use here:** relativistic and nonrelativistic Dirac/Pauli operators,
      external electromagnetic fields, supersymmetric factorization, and their
      spectral realizations.
    - **Boundary:** the uniform-field Pauli construction is a regression with
     constant-curvature and global-flatness resources; it does not establish
     decoupling for arbitrary magnetic fields.

## Adiabatic mode bundles and variable magnetic coefficients

27. G. Panati, H. Spohn, and S. Teufel, “Space-Adiabatic Perturbation Theory,”
    *Advances in Theoretical and Mathematical Physics* **7** (2003), 145--204.
    [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/math-ph/0201055)

    - **Use here:** primary construction of almost-invariant subspaces and
      effective Hamiltonians for Weyl-quantized operator-valued symbols with an
      isolated energy band.  It supplies the theorem contract behind the
      superadiabatic projector recursion.
    - **Boundary:** its symbol, gap, and regularity hypotheses must be checked for
      each PDE.  Pointwise diagonalization or informal slow variation does not
      inherit the theorem.

28. M. Dimassi, J.-C. Guillot, and J. Ralston, “On effective Hamiltonians for
    adiabatic perturbations of magnetic Schrödinger operators,” *Asymptotic
    Analysis* **40** (2004), 137--146.
    [version of record](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3233/ASY-2004-657)

    - **Use here:** evidence that slowly varying magnetic and electric
      perturbations require an effective-Hamiltonian construction adapted to the
      magnetic Schrödinger setting rather than substitution of local constant-
      field formulas.
    - **Boundary:** it does not erase Landau degeneracy, global spectral overlap,
      or the need to state the chosen phase-space/energy sector.

29. B. Simon, “Holonomy, the Quantum Adiabatic Theorem, and Berry's Phase,”
    *Physical Review Letters* **51** (1983), 2167--2170.
    [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.51.2167)

    - **Use here:** primary geometric interpretation of adiabatic phase as the
      holonomy of the connection on the eigenstate line bundle.  It supports
      retaining the bundle and connection rather than choosing globally phased
      eigenvectors.
    - **Boundary:** the geometric identification alone supplies neither the
      space-adiabatic residual estimate nor a gap where the spectrum crosses.

## Observable-cyclic and Krylov reduction

30. C. Lanczos, “An Iteration Method for the Solution of the Eigenvalue Problem
    of Linear Differential and Integral Operators,” *Journal of Research of the
    National Bureau of Standards* **45** (1950), 255--282.
    [DOI](https://doi.org/10.6028/jres.045.026)

    - **Use here:** primary source for the finite recurrence that tridiagonalizes
      a self-adjoint operator on the Krylov carrier generated by one starting
      vector.
    - **Boundary:** finite-precision loss of orthogonality and early-termination
      error are separate numerical obligations; the active bench uses exact
      termination on a finite invariant carrier.

31. G. H. Golub and G. Meurant, *Matrices, Moments and Quadrature with
    Applications*, Princeton University Press, 2010.
    [publisher chapter](https://assets.press.princeton.edu/chapters/s9104.pdf)

    - **Use here:** authoritative account of the relation among matrix-function
      bilinear forms, spectral measures, moments, orthogonal-polynomial
      recurrences, quadrature, and Lanczos reduction.
    - **Boundary:** the book supplies the numerical-linear-algebra theorem
      contract; the physical coefficient carrier and observable-preserving
     intertwiner are constructed internally from the PDE.

## Filtered centralizers and the Coulomb hidden algebra

32. M. Eastwood, “Higher Symmetries of the Laplacian,” *Annals of Mathematics*
    **161** (2005), 1645--1665.
    [DOI](https://doi.org/10.4007/annals.2005.161.1645)

    - **Use here:** primary complete model of a filtered higher-symmetry algebra:
      leading conformal-Killing-tensor data lift to differential symmetries, and
      the algebra is identified as an enveloping-algebra quotient.
    - **Boundary:** this theorem concerns the Laplacian/conformal setting; it does
      not make the filtered-centralizer lift complete for arbitrary potentials,
      bundles, or boundary realizations.

    - **Boundary:** the published classification uses a broad coordinate/system
      analysis; it motivates relative-completeness tests but is not the active

## Non-Abelian invariant-tensor construction

52. A. Rajaraman, “Invariant Tensors for `SU(3)` with Adjoints” (2022).
    [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.11851)

    - **Use here:** recent primary evidence that independent invariant tensors for
      multiple `SU(3)` adjoints form a genuine construction problem, and that a
      complete generic inventory can require invariant-theory and Lie-computation
      machinery rather than visual inspection.
    - **Boundary:** its broad Hilbert-basis computation is not imported as the
      active transition algorithm. The bounded router constructs only the two
      trilinear adjoint channels from matrix multiplication and uses source 23 as
      the tensor-product completeness contract.

53. H. E. Haber, “Useful relations among the generators in the defining and
    adjoint representations of `SU(N)`,” *SciPost Physics Lecture Notes* **21**
    (2021). [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.13302)
